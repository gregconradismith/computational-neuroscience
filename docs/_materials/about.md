Computational Neuroscience is a William & Mary COLL 400 capstone course for students who want to understand nervous systems as information-processing, dynamical, and biological systems.

The course emphasizes the second sense of computational neuroscience: not only building models of neural phenomena, but asking how nervous systems compute, represent, integrate, decide, remember, and act. We use theory as a partner to experiment, not as a substitute for it.

Major themes include Marr's levels of analysis, neural representation, physical computation, attractor dynamics, sensory coding, action selection, learning, comparative cognition, and the limits of computational explanation.

The course is seminar-like. Students read primary literature and substantial scientific or philosophical arguments, discuss them carefully, and develop projects that synthesize ideas across neuroscience, computation, and philosophy of mind.

The central habit of mind is critical modeling: ask what a model explains, what it leaves out, what assumptions it hides, and what sort of evidence would make the explanation stronger or weaker.

Students should expect to read closely, speak thoughtfully, write clearly, and revise their understanding as the semester develops.
